Platen: 2 directions

Monospaced fonts with more character than a plain coding face, in the spirit of the return to typewriter and early-computer looks that recent coding fonts made popular. A typewriter face first. - **What it is for:** letters, notes and code that should look typed: a blog in the voice of a 1960s office, a zine, a terminal theme with character. Also posters, where the typed look is the point. (I chose this. The person did not say.) - **How it should feel:** a 1960s portable typewriter. Friendly, a little round, printed black. - **What it must not look like:** Courier. It must not be thin, and it must not copy any one machine's type. - **Styles:** Light, Regular and Bold, with a weight axis. An inked companion that looks typed through a ribbon, which still reads as text.
